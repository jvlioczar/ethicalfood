Timeline

Milestones of alternative proteins

The most important news on the subject, from the first prediction of cultivated meat to the latest approvals and bans. Each event links to its source.

1931

Science

Churchill imagines growing meat

In the essay “Fifty Years Hence”, Winston Churchill predicts that people will grow chicken breasts and wings separately, instead of raising whole birds.

Source

1985

Business

Quorn reaches shops

Mycoprotein made by fermenting a fungus goes on sale in the United Kingdom under the Quorn brand, one of the first meat alternatives sold at scale.

Source

1999

Science

First patent for cultured meat

Dutch researcher Willem van Eelen obtains a patent for producing meat from cells, after decades promoting the idea.

Source

2002

Science

Fish tissue grown in the lab

A NASA-funded team led by Morris Benjaminson grows goldfish muscle tissue, studying food for long space missions.

Source

2004

Science

New Harvest is founded

Jason Matheny founds New Harvest, the first nonprofit dedicated to cultured meat research.

Source

2009

Business

Beyond Meat is founded

Ethan Brown founds Beyond Meat in California. Two years later, Patrick Brown founds Impossible Foods.

Source

2013

AugScience

The first cultivated burger

In London, Mark Post presents the first hamburger made from cultivated beef cells, tasted at a press event streamed live. It cost about €250,000 and was funded by Sergey Brin.

Source

2014

Business

Perfect Day is founded

The company that would sell the first ice cream made with whey protein produced by precision fermentation, without cows, is founded as Muufri.

Source

2015

Business

Memphis Meats is founded

One of the first cultivated meat startups in the United States is founded. It later becomes Upside Foods.

Source

2016

Science

The Good Food Institute is founded

Bruce Friedrich founds GFI, which becomes the main global nonprofit for alternative proteins, with later branches in Brazil, Asia, Europe, India and Israel.

Source
JulBusiness

The Impossible Burger debuts

A plant-based burger that “bleeds”, thanks to soy heme made by fermentation, goes on the menu at Momofuku Nishi in New York.

Source

2019

MayBusiness

Beyond Meat goes public

Beyond Meat’s stock opening is one of the strongest of the year, rising about 160% on the first day of trading.

Source
AugBusiness

The Impossible Whopper goes national

After a test in St. Louis, Burger King puts the Impossible Whopper on the menu across the United States.

Source
Business

Plant-based burgers take off in Brazil

Fazenda Futuro launches its Futuro Burger and Seara, from JBS, launches the Incrível line, bringing plant-based meat to Brazilian supermarkets.

Source

2020

DecApprovals and rules

Singapore approves cultivated meat

Singapore becomes the first country to approve the sale of cultivated meat: Eat Just’s chicken, served at the restaurant 1880 that same month.

Source

2021

MayBusiness

Oatly goes public

The Swedish oat milk maker lists on the Nasdaq, one of the largest public offerings of a plant-based food company.

Source

2022

NovApprovals and rules

First US safety clearance

The US Food and Drug Administration completes its first safety consultation on cultivated meat, for Upside Foods’ chicken.

Source

2023

JunApprovals and rules

Cultivated chicken on sale in the US

The US Department of Agriculture approves Upside Foods and Good Meat, and their cultivated chicken is served in restaurants in San Francisco and Washington.

Source
NovBans

Italy bans cultivated meat

Italy’s parliament approves a law banning the production and sale of cultivated meat, the first country to do so.

Source
DecApprovals and rules

Brazil updates its novel food rules

Anvisa publishes RDC 839/2023, which updates how new foods are approved and covers products made by fermentation or from cultured cells.

Source

2024

JanApprovals and rules

Israel approves cultivated beef

Aleph Farms announces the world’s first approval for cultivated beef, from Israel’s Ministry of Health.

Source
MayBans

Florida and Alabama ban cultivated meat

Two US states ban the sale of cultivated meat. Other states follow in the next months.

Source
JulApprovals and rules

First approval of cultivated pet food

The United Kingdom clears Meatly’s cultivated chicken for pet food, the first approval of its kind in the world and the first for cultivated meat in Europe.

Source
JulApprovals and rules

First request in the European Union

French company Gourmey files the first application to sell cultivated meat in the European Union, for duck foie gras.

Source
AugScience

Continuous production at a lower cost

A study in Nature Food by the Hebrew University and Believer Meats describes continuous production of cultivated chicken, filtering and reusing the culture medium, with an animal-free medium at 63 US cents per liter. It estimates chicken at about US$6.20 per pound in a large plant. The study was partly funded by the company.

Source

2025

FebBusiness

Cultivated meat reaches a pet shop

Dog treats made with Meatly’s cultivated chicken go on sale at a Pets at Home store in London, the first retail sale of cultivated pet food.

Source
AprScience

Artificial blood vessels for whole cuts

University of Tokyo researchers grow a piece of chicken of more than 10 grams using over a thousand hollow fibers that feed the tissue like blood vessels, a step toward whole cuts instead of minced meat.

Source
Approvals and rules

US clears cultivated pork and salmon

The FDA clears Mission Barns’ cultivated pork fat, the first cultivated pork approved anywhere, and Wildtype’s cultivated salmon.

Source
JunApprovals and rules

Australia and New Zealand approve cultivated quail

After a two-year review, Vow’s cultivated quail is approved and heads to restaurants in Melbourne and Sydney.

Source
JunBans

Texas bans cultivated meat for two years

Texas, the largest beef-producing state in the US, becomes the seventh state to ban cultivated meat. Upside Foods and Wildtype sue the state in September.

Source

2026

NovBusiness

Upside Foods reaches a million pounds a year

Upside Foods says it can produce more than 1 million pounds (about 450 tonnes) of chicken products a year, growing cells in tanks of 20,000 liters or more, and starts selling in California restaurants. The figure covers the finished product, which mixes cultivated chicken with plant ingredients.
